In this mode you click every domain node (AA site or wall crossing) you can see; there is nothing to count. With 3 or more nodes a lattice is fitted to all of them by least squares: the dashed circles are the fit carried across the field (they should sit on the nodes everywhere), and each click gets a residual line drawn 5x longer than it is; red marks a click far off its fitted node. The fit gives the three periods L1, L2, L3 (equal for pure twist; over 5 % apart means heterostrain), their mean L with an error bar, and the twist. Click a node again to remove it, drag it to adjust it; Backspace removes the last one. Lines and nodes are kept separately.

Each line you draw measures a moire domain length L; the twist angle is calculated from L. Drag from one domain node (AA site or wall crossing) to another a few domains away along a row. Set periods to the number of domains the line spans: a circle marks each period, so they should sit on the nodes. Drag again anywhere to add another line: every line is its own domain-length measurement (new lines start at 1 period, so one line across one domain is one length), and the image shows their mean, spread and count. Click a line to select it, drag its ends to adjust; Shift-drag always starts a new line, even on top of another. Arrow keys change image, + and - change periods, Delete removes the selected line.

Each line measures a domain length L = length / periods; each line is a separate measurement; an image's L is the mean of its lines (with the standard deviation). The twist is then calculated from that AA-to-AA moire period L of a homobilayer: theta = 2 asin(a / 2L). In node mode the image L is the mean of the three periods of a lattice fitted to all clicked nodes. Heterobilayers (lattice mismatch) are not modelled; heterostrain shows as unequal periods. Lines are kept in this browser (localStorage) by image name; images are never stored or sent anywhere.

In this mode you click every domain node (AA site or wall crossing) you can see; there is nothing to count. Neighbouring nodes are joined into triangular moire cells; each cell's three sides L1 ≤ L2 ≤ L3 give its local twist and heterostrain (a twisted bilayer with uniaxial heterostrain, solved exactly; the cell colour is its twist). The image result is the median cell. Unequal sides are normal: heterostrain stretches the cells, and the pure-twist formula on the mean L then reads the twist wrong. Dashed grey triangles are not counted (thin slivers at the edge, or a gap where a node is missing); a red node is not in any cell. Click a node again to remove it, drag to adjust; Backspace removes the last one. Lines and nodes are kept separately.

Each line you draw measures a moire domain length L; the twist angle is calculated from L. Drag from one domain node (AA site or wall crossing) to another a few domains away along a row. Set periods to the number of domains the line spans: a circle marks each period, so they should sit on the nodes. Drag again anywhere to add another line: every line is its own domain-length measurement (new lines start at 1 period, so one line across one domain is one length), and the image shows their mean, spread and count. Click a line to select it, drag its ends to adjust; Shift-drag always starts a new line, even on top of another. Arrow keys change image, + and - change periods, Delete removes the selected line.

Each line measures a domain length L = length / periods; each line is a separate measurement; an image's L is the mean of its lines (with the standard deviation). The twist is then calculated from that AA-to-AA moire period L of a homobilayer: theta = 2 asin(a / 2L). In node mode the clicked nodes are joined into triangular cells and each cell is solved for twist and uniaxial heterostrain; the image takes the median cell. Heterobilayers (lattice mismatch) are not modelled; heterostrain shows as unequal periods. Lines are kept in this browser (localStorage) by image name; images are never stored or sent anywhere.
